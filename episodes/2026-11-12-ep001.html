Anduril says its first satellite can see adversaries maneuver in orbital night

Alex Anduril is claiming they’ve demonstrated a satellite that can track spacecraft even when they’re in Earth’s shadow. Ars Technica has the story.

Maya Wait, really? Tracking spacecraft?

Alex Yeah, apparently. It's their satellite, Anduril-216, orbiting about 590 kilometers up.

Maya That’s a lot of hardware for what sounds like a niche problem.

Alex It's a tech demo, funded internally. They launched it on a SpaceX rideshare mission back in July.

Maya And what exactly is it doing up there?

Alex It's got an infrared imager that works during those dark periods—about a third of each orbit. They’re using it to spot other objects.

Maya So, just an edge-compute processor, power management, and this infrared sensor. Seems like a focused payload.

Alex Exactly. They’re saying it can see maneuvers others might be trying to hide.

Taiwan’s chipmaking giant TSMC to invest $1B in Anthropic, OpenAI

Maya So, TSMC, the biggest chipmaker around, is putting a billion dollars into OpenAI and Anthropic. That’s the surprising part, isn't it?

Alex I thought they were just happily selling chips to those companies.

Maya They’ve already made a ton of money supplying chips for AI data centers. This investment is up to 500 million in OpenAI and another 500 in Anthropic.

Alex That's a lot. What's the breakdown – preferred stock for OpenAI, common shares for Anthropic?

Maya Yep. Apparently, the board approved it all. It's a bit of a move to give the AI market a boost, according to Ars Technica.

Alex I wonder how much say they’ll actually have. Are they just throwing money or expecting something in return?

Maya Analysts are concerned it makes it harder to see what’s actually driving demand for AI hardware and services, you know, with this kind of circular investment.

Alex Circular financing… yeah, I can see why that's tricky to value.

Maya It is.

All 7 Anthropic founders will each cash out about $250M in IPO

Alex Maya, did you see this TechCrunch piece about Anthropic’s IPO? Seems like the founders are all taking a pretty significant payday.

Maya $250 million each, apparently.

Alex Yeah, each selling just over three million shares. It’s interesting they’re all selling the same amount, though.

Maya It does suggest some level of coordination. They’ll still own about 3.2% of Class A shares each, collectively 22%.

Alex They’re still in control?

Maya They’ll have 51.1% of the votes, thanks to Class F stock. Amazon and Google also have shares, but of a different class.

Alex So, they left OpenAI to focus on AI safety, and now they’re getting rich.

Maya That’s the story, yes. Reed Hastings wants in too, apparently.

ChatGPT is getting verified AI ‘Business Agents’

Maya OpenAI's letting businesses create AI agents to represent them within ChatGPT.

Alex Like, full-on brand reps?

Maya Apparently. The Verge reports it’s starting as a beta program for e-commerce and online services in the US.

Alex That's a lot of businesses.

Maya Businesses can specify instructions and context for these agents, so they can answer questions about their products.

Alex So, comparison shopping, basically?

Maya That’s the main use case they're highlighting, yes. And there's this thing called Business Hub for verification.

Alex Verification? How do they even do that?

Meta head of infrastructure will leave and be succeeded by former AWS cloud exec

Alex This one caught my eye because it’s a pretty significant shift in Meta’s infrastructure leadership – their head of infrastructure, Santosh Janardhan, is leaving.

Maya Leaving? Seriously? That’s a lot of experience walking out the door.

Alex Apparently he’s handing over the reins to David Brown, who just joined from AWS in July.

Maya Hold on, so he’s only been there a few months? That’s a quick transition.

Alex Yeah, Janardhan will support Brown through the first quarter of 2027, so it's not cold turkey. CNBC reports he said it's “the right time.”

Maya Right. And this is all part of Mark Zuckerberg’s push to expand data center operations, apparently.

Alex Seems like it’s linked to investor pressure regarding capital spending, too.

Maya Which, given their negative free cash flow, makes sense.

Alex Exactly.

Jim Cramer says investors need to pay attention to CoreWeave. Here's why

Maya Apparently Jim Cramer is warming up to CoreWeave, according to CNBC. Their stock surged nearly 25% after they released earnings and the CEO had a chat with him.

Alex Cramer? I thought he’d be more skeptical.

Maya Yeah, he was, but he’s changed his tune. They’re seeing demand ten times greater than they can supply, which is allowing them to increase prices.

Alex Makes you wonder how sustainable that is, though.

Maya They’re also dealing with a significant debt load, which has been weighing down the stock, but the surge helped a bit.

Alex So, they're financing with equipment and contracts now, huh? Interesting pivot.

Maya Apparently the CEO was tempted to buy back their own bonds on the open market, but he figures building data centers is the better return.

Alex And the Nvidia chips? They're holding their value longer than people thought.
